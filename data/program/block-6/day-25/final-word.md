## A Final Word

Our Ephesians study introduced the New Human Project through the creation of one new humanity in Christ (Ephesians 2:14–16). Galatians and Colossians do not simply repeat that phrase or address identical situations. Instead, they illuminate related questions about belonging, freedom, Christ’s sufficiency, and the shape of renewed life together.

Galatians insists that the gospel is centred on Christ and cannot be replaced by a message that makes human credentials the basis of belonging or righteousness. It moves from the cross and faith into freedom, life by the Spirit, love, restoration, and new creation.

Colossians presents Christ in relation to creation, reconciliation, the church, resurrection, and the believer's new life. It moves from the fullness of Christ into practical transformation: putting off old patterns, putting on new character, reshaping households, praying for mission, and living wisely among outsiders.

Taken together, the letters connect belief and shared practice. Galatians 3:26–29 names belonging and unity in Christ; Colossians 3:9–17 names the new self and the habits of a community clothed in love. These passages help our church speak about A New Humanity while allowing each text to retain its own voice.

The final question is therefore simple:

_If the gospel Paul defends is true, and if Christ is who Colossians says he is, how should our shared life embody the New Human Project?_

## Suggested group rhythm

- Read the day's passage aloud before discussing it.

- Ask participants to identify what the text explicitly says before moving to interpretation.

- When traditions differ, distinguish what the passage states from conclusions drawn from it.

- Use the Reflection question for discussion and the Today's Practice for concrete response.

- End by praying the language of the passage rather than only discussing it.
