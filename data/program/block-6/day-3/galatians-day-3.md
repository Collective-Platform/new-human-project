## Today's Focus

What happened when Paul laid his gospel before the recognized leaders in Jerusalem?

## Reading Notes

Paul describes returning to Jerusalem with Barnabas and Titus. During this trip, Paul offers proof that the gospel he preaches is the same as the one taught by the 12 apostles. Titus, a loyal and effective disciple of Paul who was an uncircumcised Greek was proof of his effective ministry. Paul stands firm on his stance against the Jews that circumcision isn’t a requirement for salvation, but only by grace through faith. The leaders in Jerusalem also agreed with Paul that all of them were equally commissioned by God to preach the message, eventually agreeing on a partnership with Paul and Barnabas to continue their flourishing ministry to the Gentiles.

## Key Idea

Titus was not compelled to be circumcised. Paul describes recognition of his mission to Gentiles and a shared concern for the poor. For our New Human Project theme, the passage invites attention to belonging and partnership without erasing the particular dispute Paul recounts.

The passage presents both continuity and distinction: Paul defends the gospel he received while describing genuine recognition and partnership among different apostolic ministries.

## Reflection

What can this passage teach about staying true to our convictions while still acknowledging that God uses his people in different ways?

## Today's Practice

Based on your current beliefs on church and ministry, reflect on how it affects your relationships with others who may have slightly different beliefs from you. Ask God for clarity without suspicion and partnership without compromise.
