**Ecclesiastes 4:9–10 (NIV)**

Two are better than one, because they have a good return for their labour: if either of them falls down, one can help the other up. But pity anyone who falls and has no one to help them up.

## Reading Notes

When you choose to stay connected—to God, to people, to truth—you begin to see beyond your own limited view. Left alone, our thoughts can become loud, narrow, and sometimes even distorted. We start to believe things that feel true, but may not actually be true. But connection gently interrupts that cycle.

When you are connected to God, His Word realigns your heart. **His voice reminds you of what is eternal, not just what is urgent.** Suddenly, what felt overwhelming begins to find its place. What felt confusing begins to make sense. Not because your situation immediately changes—but because your perspective does.

When you are connected to the right people, you borrow the strength and clarity you may not have on your own. Someone speaks hope when you feel discouraged. Someone reflects the truth when you are doubting. Someone listens, and in that safe space, your thoughts begin to untangle. **Connection doesn’t just comfort you—it corrects you, grounds you, and lifts your sight.**

So the question is not just, “Am I going through something?” The deeper question is, “Am I going through it alone?”

Because isolation magnifies problems, but connection reframes them.  
**Connection brings perspective—and sometimes, perspective is the very breakthrough you’ve been praying for.**

When you walk with others, you don’t just share burdens—you gain strength.

## Practice

Reach out intentionally.

## Reflection

Who needs connection today?
