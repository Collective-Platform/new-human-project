**Mark 1:35–38**

**35** Very early in the morning, while it was still dark, Jesus got up, left the house and went off to a solitary place, where he prayed. **36** Simon and his companions went to look for him, **37** and when they found him, they exclaimed: “Everyone is looking for you\!” **38** Jesus replied, “Let us go somewhere else—to the nearby villages—so I can preach there also. That is why I have come.”

## Reading Notes

You cannot say YES to everything

Jesus loved people deeply, yet He did not respond to every demand placed upon Him.

In Mark 1, crowds were looking for Jesus. Yet He had withdrawn to a quiet place to pray. When the disciples found Him and said, **“Everyone is looking for you\!”**, Jesus did not immediately return. He knew where the Father was leading Him next.

Jesus shows us that **love does not mean unlimited availability**.

Sometimes we say yes because we fear disappointing people. We feel guilty when we rest, uncomfortable saying no, or responsible for keeping everyone happy.

Over time, this can produce exhaustion, resentment, and emotional burnout.

Healthy boundaries help us recognise: **What is mine to carry? What is not?**

A boundary is not simply pushing people away. It is knowing your limits so you can love others from a healthy place.

The Holy Spirit helps us discern when to give, when to rest, when to speak, and when to say no. You don't need to meet every need to prove that you care.

Even Jesus stepped away from people's demands to remain connected to His Father.

## Practice

Think about one commitment currently draining you. Ask: **“Did God actually ask me to carry this, or am I afraid to say no?”**

## Reflection

Why is saying “no” difficult for you?
