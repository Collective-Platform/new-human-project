## Today's Focus

What does Jesus's response to children, and his use of them as a model, reveal about the values of his Kingdom?

## Reading Notes

People were bringing children to Jesus to have him touch them, and the disciples rebuked them. The disciples were not being cruel; they were being efficient. Children had no social status, no contribution to make, no obvious reason to take up the teacher's time. And Jesus was outraged. The word Mark uses, _aganakteo,_ describes a sharp, emotional reaction. He was angry at what his disciples had done. And then he does something that must have been startling: he takes the children in his arms, blesses them, and declares: Truly, I say to you, whoever does not receive the kingdom of God like a child shall not enter it. In Matthew 18, when the disciples ask who is greatest in the kingdom of heaven, Jesus places a child in the middle of them and says: unless you turn and become like children, you will never enter the kingdom of heaven. The kingdom operates by entirely different values from the world. In the world, status comes from what you can produce, contribute, and achieve. In the kingdom, the one who receives like a child, in dependence, without status, without claim, is the greatest. Jesus consistently lifts up those whom the world overlooks and asks his disciples to look again.

## Key Idea

_Jesus placed a child in the centre of the question about greatness. The Kingdom belongs to those who receive it with the empty hands of a child not the full hands of someone who feels they have earned their place._

## Reflection

Where in your relationship with God are you tempted to come with full hands, with your accomplishments and service, rather than the empty hands of a child? What would it mean to receive your place in the Kingdom rather than achieve it?

## Today's Practice

Spend time today in prayer with the posture of a child, no agenda, no requests to process, no spiritual accomplishments to present. Simply come. Sit. Receive. Let the session be marked by receptivity rather than activity.
