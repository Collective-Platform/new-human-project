## Today's Focus

What do the resurrection appearances of Jesus reveal about the kind of risen Lord he is and what does the ascension accomplish?

## Reading Notes

The risen Jesus appears to his disciples in ways that are unmistakably him and unmistakably new. On the road to Emmaus, he walks alongside two disciples who do not recognise him, opens the Scriptures to them, and is made known in the breaking of bread, then vanishes. On the beach in John 21, he cooks breakfast and asks Peter three times: do you love me? Once for every denial. Peter is not let off the hook, but he is fully restored. The appearances are not a series of proofs delivered at a distance. They are personal, relational encounters: Jesus meeting individuals in the specific places of their grief, their doubt, and their failure. And then the ascension. Jesus is lifted up, and a cloud takes him from their sight. Two men in white ask: why do you stand looking into heaven? This Jesus, who was taken up from you into heaven, will come in the same way as you saw him go. The ascension is not Jesus disappearing. It is Jesus taking his place at the right hand of the Father as the exalted Lord, from which position he pours out the Spirit, intercedes for his people, and will one day return. The disciples go back to Jerusalem not with loss, but with great joy.

## Key Idea

_The risen Jesus meets people in the specific place of their pain and failure cooking breakfast for the discouraged, restoring the denier by name. The ascension does not end this presence. It universalises it._

## Reflection

Where is Jesus meeting you in the specific place of your own failure, grief, or doubt? What does the restoration of Peter, three questions for three denials say about how the risen Lord deals with those who have let him down?

## Today's Practice

Read John 21:15-17 slowly, substituting your own name for Peter's. Hear the three questions as addressed to you. Answer them honestly. Then receive the commission that follows the restoration: feed my sheep. Ask what feeding the sheep looks like in your specific place.
