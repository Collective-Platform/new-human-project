## Today's Focus

What is happening in Gethsemane and what does Jesus's prayer reveal about his humanity, his obedience, and the cost of what he is about to do?

## Reading Notes

They came to a place called Gethsemane, and he said to his disciples: Sit here while I pray. And he took with him Peter and James and John, and began to be greatly distressed and troubled. And he said to them: My soul is very sorrowful, even to death. Jesus is not performing courage in Gethsemane. He is not modelling stoic acceptance. He is in genuine anguish: distressed, troubled, sorrowful to the point of death. The writer of Hebrews says he offered prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears, to those who were able to save him from death. He prays: Abba, Father, all things are possible for you. Remove this cup from me. Yet not what I will, but what you will. The prayer is honest before it is obedient. He does not pretend the cost is nothing. He does not go to the cross with a smile that denies what it will cost. He feels it fully, and he chooses it anyway. This is not the compliance of a man who has no choice. It is the obedience of a son who, having felt the full weight of what he is being asked, says yes to the Father. Three times he returns to the same prayer. Three times the answer is the same. And he rises and says: Rise, let us be going; see, my betrayer is at hand.

## Key Idea

_Jesus did not go to the cross with detachment. He felt the full weight of it and chose it anyway. His obedience was not the absence of agony. It was the decision made in the middle of it._

## Reflection

Is there something God is asking of you that you have been avoiding because the cost is real? What does Gethsemane say about the relationship between honest acknowledgment of cost and genuine obedience?

## Today's Practice

Pray Gethsemane's prayer today in your own life. Name honestly what you fear or resist, holding nothing back. Then say: yet not what I will, but what you will. Do not rush the first part to reach the second. Both belong.
