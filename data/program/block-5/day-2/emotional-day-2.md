**Luke 10:38-42**

She came to him and asked, “Lord, don’t you care that my sister has left me to do the work by myself? Tell her to help me\!” **41** “Martha, Martha,” the Lord answered, “you are worried and upset about many things, **42** but few things are needed—or indeed only one.\[[f](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Luke%2010&version=NIV#fen-NIV-25406f)\] Mary has chosen what is better, and it will not be taken away from her.”

## Reading Notes

In Luke 10, Jesus enters the home of two sisters—Martha and Mary. Martha immediately gets busy. There are things to prepare. People to serve. Responsibilities to handle. Mary, however, sits at Jesus’ feet and listens.

Eventually, Martha becomes frustrated: “Lord, don’t you care that my sister has left me to do the work by myself?” Luke 10:40

Jesus responds gently: “Martha, Martha, you are worried and upset about many things, but few things are needed—or indeed only one.”  Luke 10:41–42

Jesus wasn't saying that serving was wrong. The problem wasn't simply that Martha was **busy**. Something was happening **inside her busyness**.

She was distracted. Worried. Upset. And eventually, her inner unrest affected the way she saw Mary—and even the way she spoke to Jesus.

**This is what hurry can do to us.**

We may look productive on the outside while becoming increasingly restless on the inside.

## Practice

Sometime today, intentionally stop what you're doing for three minutes. Put your phone aside. Be present with God.

## Reflection

Would you describe your current life as busy, hurried, or both?
