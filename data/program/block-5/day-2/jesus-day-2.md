## Today's Focus

What does God declare about Jesus at his baptism and transfiguration and why does the Father's voice matter so much?

## Reading Notes

Twice in the Gospels, the voice of the Father breaks through from heaven to identify Jesus. At his baptism, as Jesus comes up from the water, the Spirit descends like a dove and the voice speaks: This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased. At the transfiguration, as Jesus is transformed before Peter, James, and John on the mountain, the voice comes again from the bright cloud: This is my beloved Son, with whom I am well pleased; listen to him. Both declarations carry enormous weight. The baptism declaration comes before Jesus has performed a single miracle or spoken a single public word: the Father's delight in his Son is not based on performance. It is based on relationship. The transfiguration declaration comes at the midpoint of the ministry, when Jesus has set his face toward Jerusalem and the cross: and the Father speaks into it with the same words: beloved, well pleased. And adds one instruction: listen to him. The whole of Jesus's ministry is bracketed by the Father's voice. He does not go out to earn his identity; he goes out from it.

## Key Idea

_The Father declared Jesus beloved before the ministry began and at its turning point. Jesus did not work to earn the Father's approval, he worked from it. This is the pattern for every child of God._

## Reflection

How much of your own relationship with God is shaped by the need to earn his approval rather than to live from it? What would it look like to receive the same declaration? Beloved, well pleased as the ground you stand on rather than the goal you reach for?

## Today's Practice

Read Romans 8:15-17 alongside Matthew 3:17. Write your name into the declaration: '\[Your name\] is my beloved child, in whom I am well pleased.' Say it out loud. Ask the Spirit to make it more real to you than whatever performance-based identity you habitually carry.
