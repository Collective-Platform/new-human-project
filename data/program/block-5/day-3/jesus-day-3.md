## Today's Focus

What does Jesus mean when he calls himself the Son of Man — and why is this title more significant than it first sounds?

## Reading Notes

Son of Man is the title Jesus uses most often of himself, and it is the one most easily misunderstood as a statement of humility alone. In first century Jewish ears, the phrase carried the weight of Daniel 7:13-14, where one like a son of man comes on the clouds of heaven to the Ancient of Days and receives dominion, glory, and a kingdom that all peoples, nations, and languages should serve him. When Jesus forgives the paralyzed man's sins in Mark 2, the scribes immediately recognize the claim he is making: who can forgive sins but God alone? Jesus does not back down. He heals the man and declares: the Son of Man has authority on earth to forgive sins. He is not claiming merely human authority, but the authority of the one Daniel saw: the figure who comes from heaven, to whom all dominion belongs. The title Son of Man is simultaneously the most human sounding and the most exalted claim Jesus makes. He is the human being who is also the heavenly Lord: the one in whom humanity and divinity meet without confusion.

## Key Idea

_When Jesus called himself the Son of Man, he was not diminishing his identity. He was claiming the authority of Daniel's heavenly figure; the one to whom all dominion belongs._

## Reflection

When you realize "Son of Man" means Jesus has both total divine authority and full human experience, how does that change the way you trust his leadership in your life?

## Today's Practice

Read Daniel 7:13-14 and then Mark 2:1-12. Write down the specific authority Jesus claims in the healing account and trace it back to Daniel's vision. Then ask yourself: is there an area of my life I have not yet brought under this authority? Name it and pray about it.
