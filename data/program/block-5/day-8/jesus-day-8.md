## Today's Focus

Why does Jesus consistently move toward those on the margins and what does his welcome of them reveal about the nature of the Kingdom?

## Reading Notes

Two scenes, separated by geography and culture, tell the same story. In Luke 5, Jesus walks past a tax collector's booth and says to Levi: follow me. Tax collectors were despised as collaborators with Roman occupiers, men who grew rich by extracting more than was owed. They were social and religious outsiders. Jesus invites one to dinner, and a crowd of tax collectors and sinners gathers. When the Pharisees complain, Jesus answers: those who are well have no need of a physician, but those who are sick. I have not come to call the righteous but sinners to repentance. In John 4, Jesus sits at a well in Samaria, crossing a cultural boundary, and speaks to a Samaritan woman. She is a double outsider: Samaritan by ethnicity, and a woman with a complicated personal history. Jesus initiates the conversation, asks her for water, and leads her into one of the deepest theological exchanges in the Gospels. She becomes the first person in John to whom Jesus explicitly declares his identity as Messiah, and the first evangelist in the book, going back to her city to tell what she has heard. The pattern is clear and deliberate: Jesus moves toward those the religious culture of his day moved away from.

## Key Idea

_Jesus did not wait for the outsiders to clean up and come to him. He moved toward them which means that wherever the margins are, that is where he is going._

## Reflection

Who are the outsiders in your community? The people your culture, your church, or your instincts tend to move away from? What would it look like to deliberately move toward them the way Jesus moved toward Levi and the Samaritan woman?

## Today's Practice

Identify one person in your sphere of life who feels like an outsider, to your community, your faith, your social world. Take one step toward them this week that is not about having an agenda for them, but simply about being present with them as Jesus was present.
