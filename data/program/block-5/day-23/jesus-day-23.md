## Today's Focus

What does the resurrection of Jesus mean and what are its consequences for everything that follows?

## Reading Notes

On the first day of the week, Mary Magdalene comes to the tomb while it is still dark and finds the stone rolled away. She runs. Peter and the other disciple run. The other disciple reaches the tomb first, looks in, and sees the linen cloth lying there. Then Peter goes in and sees the face cloth folded and set apart. The detail of the folded cloth is the detail of someone who left in order, not unwrapped by resurrection as a confused emergency, but risen. The other disciple saw and believed. Mary stands outside weeping, looks in, and hears someone say her name: Mary. She turns and says: Rabboni. The resurrection is not a resuscitation, Jesus does not return to the life he had before. He rises to a new order of life, the life of the age to come, bodily and real but no longer subject to death. Paul stakes everything on it: if Christ has not been raised, your faith is futile and you are still in your sins. And: if Christ has not been raised, those who have fallen asleep in Christ have perished. But Christ has been raised, the firstfruits of those who have fallen asleep. The resurrection is not the happy ending of a story. It is the beginning of the new creation, breaking into the present age, guaranteeing that everything Jesus accomplished at the cross is valid, effective, and permanent.

## Key Idea

_The resurrection is not a spiritual metaphor it is the bodily rising of Jesus as the firstfruits of the new creation. Everything the gospel promises is secured by an empty tomb._

## Reflection

Paul says that if the resurrection is not true, our faith is futile. What does it mean for your daily life, not just your theology, that it is true? How would you live differently today if the resurrection were the most real thing in your world?

## Today's Practice

Read 1 Corinthians 15:20-22 and then 1 Corinthians 15:58. The resurrection produces a specific posture: therefore, my beloved brothers, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that in the Lord your labour is not in vain. Write down one piece of work, one investment of love, one act of faithfulness that feels uncertain or unrewarded. Hold it up to the resurrection and receive the promise that it is not in vain.
