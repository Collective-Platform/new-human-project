## Today's Focus

What is Jesus doing in the entry into Jerusalem and the institution of the Lord's Supper and what is he asking his disciples to understand?

## Reading Notes

Jesus enters Jerusalem on a donkey, deliberately fulfilling Zechariah 9:9: Rejoice greatly, O daughter of Zion\! Behold, your king is coming to you; righteous and having salvation is he, humble and mounted on a donkey. The crowds lay down cloaks and branches and cry: Hosanna\! Blessed is he who comes in the name of the Lord\! But the welcome is built on a misunderstanding. They are expecting a military liberator, a king who will overthrow Rome. What Jesus is actually doing is announcing a kingdom that will be established not by military conquest, but by his own death. The last supper, held in the upper room, reframes everything. As Jesus takes the bread and breaks it and says this is my body, given for you, and takes the cup and says this cup is the new covenant in my blood, he is placing the cross at the centre of the covenant meal. The Passover, which commemorated deliverance from Egypt, is now being fulfilled in a greater deliverance. Do this in remembrance of me: not merely as a memorial of a past event, but as a proclamation of a present reality, the body broken and the blood poured out for the forgiveness of sins.

## Key Idea

_Jesus entered Jerusalem knowing exactly what was ahead and he went anyway. The last supper was his way of making sure his disciples understood why. The cross was not an accident. It was the plan._

## Reflection

What does it mean to you that Jesus entered Jerusalem deliberately, knowing the cross was waiting? What does his willingness to go say about the nature of his love for you specifically?

## Today's Practice

The next time you take communion, whether this week or the next time your community gathers, receive it with the deliberate awareness of what Jesus said: this is my body, given for you. Let the 'for you' be personal. Let the bread and cup be more than a habit.
