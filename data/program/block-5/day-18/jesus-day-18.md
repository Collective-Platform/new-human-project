## Today's Focus

What is new about the new commandment and what does Jesus mean when he says love one another as I have loved you?

## Reading Notes

On the night of his betrayal, after Judas has left, Jesus speaks to his remaining disciples: A new commandment I give to you, that you love one another: just as I have loved you, you also are to love one another. By this all people will know that you are my disciples, if you have love for one another. The command to love was not new. Leviticus 19:18 had already said love your neighbour as yourself. What is new is the standard: as I have loved you. Not as you love yourself, which sets the bar at self interest, but as Jesus has loved them. How has Jesus loved them? He has washed their feet. He is about to lay down his life. This is the love that defines the new community: not affection that goes where it feels welcome, not generosity that expects return, but the love that serves without condition, that goes to the cross for those who do not deserve it, that lays down its life for its friends. Then Jesus makes the standard even more explicit: Greater love has no one than this, that someone lay down his life for his friends. This is not sentimental love. It is the most demanding standard imaginable, and it is the mark by which the world will recognise the new humanity.

## Key Idea

The new commandment goes beyond loving your neighbour as yourself. It calls us to love one another as Jesus loved us. The standard is set by his sacrificial love on the cross, and everything else simply builds on that truth.

## Reflection

What would it look like in your relationships to love like Jesus: serving without condition, giving without expecting return, and laying something down for another?

## Today's Practice

Pick one relationship where loving like Jesus costs you something. Take one real step toward that love today. Do not wait until you feel like it. Act first, and ask the Spirit to guide your heart after.
