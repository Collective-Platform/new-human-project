## Today's Focus

What does the repeated compassion of Jesus reveal about the heart of God toward human suffering?

## Reading Notes

Three scenes. Jesus sees the crowds, harassed and helpless, like sheep without a shepherd, and he has compassion on them. A leper comes and kneels: if you will, you can make me clean. Jesus is moved with compassion. He stretches out his hand and touches him, the touch that no one in the crowd would give, the touch that would make Jesus ceremonially unclean, and says: I will; be clean. In Nain, Jesus encounters a funeral, a widow's only son being carried out. When the Lord saw her, he had compassion on her and said: do not weep. He touches the bier and says: young man, I say to you, arise. The word the Gospels use for compassion, _splagchnizomai,_ comes from the word for the organs of the body. It describes a feeling so deep it is felt physically. This is not the polite sympathy of an observer. It is the gut level response of someone who feels the suffering of others personally. The compassion of Jesus is not performed for the crowd. It moves him to action before thinking through the implications. He heals the leper knowing it will bring him unwanted attention. He stops a funeral procession in the middle of a crowd because a widow is weeping.

## Key Idea

_The compassion of Jesus is not polite or theoretical, it is physical, immediate, and moves him to action. In every scene of suffering he encounters, his response is the same: he is moved, and he moves._

## Reflection

When you encounter the suffering of others in your community, your city, the world, what is your instinctive response? Avoidance? Management? Or the kind of compassion that moves you toward rather than away? What would it look like to have the compassion of Jesus formed in you?

## Today's Practice

Today, pay attention to one person or situation you encounter that involves suffering or need. Do not manage it from a distance. Ask God for the compassion of Jesus, felt deeply enough to produce movement. Then take one step toward, however small.
