## Today's Focus

What do the hard sayings of Jesus reveal about the cost of following him, and how should we handle the parts of his teaching we find most difficult?

## Reading Notes

Jesus said things that caused people to leave. After his teaching about eating his flesh and drinking his blood in John 6, many disciples said: this is a hard saying, who can listen to it? Many drew back and no longer walked with him. Jesus did not chase them down or soften the saying. He turned to the twelve and asked: do you want to go away as well? Simon Peter answered like a man with nowhere else to go: Lord, to whom shall we go? You have the words of eternal life. In Mark 8, Jesus tells his disciples what following him will cost: if anyone would come after me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow me. Whoever would save his life will lose it, but whoever loses his life for my sake and the gospel will save it. In Luke 9, would be followers offer conditions: let me first bury my father, let me first say farewell. Jesus's responses sound uncomfortably hard. They are. The hard sayings of Jesus are not mistakes or exaggerations. They are the honest description of what genuine discipleship involves, and the measure of what following him is worth.

## Key Idea

_Jesus did not soften his teaching when it caused people to leave. To follow him honestly is to sit with the hard sayings until we find, like Peter, that there is nowhere else to go._

## Reflection

What is the hardest saying of Jesus for you personally, the teaching you most wish he had not said, or had said differently? What would it mean to sit with it honestly rather than explaining it away?

## Today's Practice

Take the hard saying that most challenges you and read it in its full context. Do not try to resolve it today. Simply bring it to Jesus with Peter's words: Lord, to whom shall I go? You have the words of eternal life. Ask him to open it to you over time.
