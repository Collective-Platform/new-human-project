## Today's Focus

What is John claiming in the opening of his Gospel and why does it matter that the Word became flesh?

## Reading Notes

In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. John opens his Gospel echoing Genesis 1:1 with an immediate claim of staggering scope: Jesus existed before creation, was with God in the beginning, and was himself God. The Word, logos, is not merely a communication from God but a person who is God. Then comes the claim that centres the entire Gospel: the Word became flesh and dwelt among us, and we have seen his glory. The Greek word for dwelt is eskēnōsen: he tabernacled, he pitched his tent among us. The deliberate language shows that the glory filling the wilderness tabernacle and Jerusalem temple has now taken up residence in a human body. God has not sent a message; he has come himself. What those who saw him witnessed was full of grace and truth, the two qualities defining the character of the God of Israel throughout the Old Testament, now visible in a human face. The incarnation is not a stepping down of God, but the fullest possible expression of who he has always been.

## Key Idea

_The Word did not become flesh reluctantly or temporarily. The eternal Son of God took on human nature permanently and in doing so, brought the full character of God within human reach._

## Reflection

What does it mean to you personally that God did not remain at a distance but became flesh and that he knows hunger, fatigue, grief, and temptation from the inside? How does the incarnation change the way you approach him?

## Today's Practice

Read John 1:1-18 slowly, twice. The first time, focus on what it says about who Jesus is. The second time, focus on what it says about who you are in relation to him; verse 12 in particular. Write one sentence in response to each reading.
