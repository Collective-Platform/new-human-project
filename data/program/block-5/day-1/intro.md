## A Word Before You Begin

Every volume of the New Human Project has been, in one way or another, about Jesus. Ephesians showed us what God has done in Christ to create a new humanity. Philippians showed us what it looks like to live that life under pressure, from the mind of Christ. The Holy Spirit showed us the one who forms Christ in us. Romans showed us the gospel through which Christ's righteousness becomes ours. Genesis showed us the long story that made Christ's coming necessary and inevitable.

Now we come to Jesus himself.

Not to a theme, not to a doctrine, but to a person. The most remarkable, most disruptive, most grace-filled person who has ever walked on the earth. These 25 days are an attempt to attend to him carefully: to sit with the Gospel accounts, to read them with fresh eyes, and to allow the person of Jesus to be as large and as surprising and as demanding as he actually is.

This study draws from all four Gospels, from the letters of Paul, from Hebrews, and from the Old Testament passages that Jesus himself treated as the foundation of his identity and mission. The approach is the same as in every volume of this series: evangelical, text-centred, and without theological agenda beyond following where the text leads.

The four movements follow a natural order. We begin with who Jesus is because everything else depends on getting this right. We then move to how he lived, because his identity was not an abstraction but a daily, embodied reality. We then listen to what he taught, not to extract principles but to be reoriented by the voice that astonished the crowds and still does. And we end where the story always ends and always begins again: the cross, the empty tomb, and the commission to carry what we have received into the world.

Jesus asked his disciples one question that he asks every person who spends time with him: Who do you say that I am? These 25 days are preparation for a better answer to that question than the one you began with. And better than any answer is this: the willingness to keep sitting with him until the question itself opens into something deeper than an answer \- into knowing, and being known.
