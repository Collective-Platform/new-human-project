## Today's Focus

What does Jesus's response to the storm on the lake reveal about his authority over the creation he made and about the nature of fear and faith?

## Reading Notes

The disciples are experienced fishermen who know the Sea of Galilee. When they are afraid on the water, they are afraid for good reason: the storm is dangerous. Jesus is asleep in the stern, on a cushion. The detail of the cushion is one of those small, humanizing touches that only an eyewitness includes. The disciples wake him: Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing? He woke and rebuked the wind and said to the sea: Peace\! Be still\! And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm. Then he turns to the disciples: Why are you so afraid? Have you still no faith? Their response is awe: Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him? The question the storm produces is the question every miracle in the Gospels is designed to press: who is this? The answer the whole Gospel builds toward: he is the one through whom all things were created, the one in whom all things hold together. He rebukes the storm not as one managing it from outside, but as one who made it, sustains it, and can still it with a word. The same Jesus who slept on the cushion commands the sea, and both are equally real.

## Key Idea

_The one who slept in the storm is the one who stilled it. The full humanity and the sovereign authority of Jesus occupy the same boat and he is in your boat too._

## Reflection

What storm are you currently facing? Does Jesus feel asleep in the middle of it, or present and in control? When fear tells you you are sinking, what does this story remind you about the difference between how things feel and what is actually true?

## Today's Practice

Write down the storm you are most afraid of right now the situation that feels most out of control. Then write the words Jesus spoke: Peace. Be still. Pray them over the storm. Not as a magic formula, but as an act of trust in the one who has authority over it.
