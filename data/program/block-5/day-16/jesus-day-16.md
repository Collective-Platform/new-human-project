## Today's Focus

What is Jesus doing in the parables and why does Luke 15 gather three of them together as a response to one complaint?

## Reading Notes

The three parables of Luke 15, the lost sheep, the lost coin, and the lost son, respond to a specific situation: the Pharisees and scribes grumbling that Jesus receives sinners and eats with them. Jesus does not defend himself. He tells three stories. Each one is about something lost, the effort of searching, and the celebration when what was lost is found. The third parable, the prodigal son, is the longest and most revealing. A younger son demands his inheritance early, wastes it in reckless living, comes to his senses in a pig pen, and returns rehearsing a speech. The father sees him a long way off, which means he has been watching the road, and runs to embrace and kiss him. Before the son finishes his speech, the robe, the ring, and the sandals are brought out. Then the older son, who stayed and worked faithfully, refused to enter the celebration. The father goes out to him too, offering the same invitation. The story ends without telling us if the older son enters the party. The invitation remains open. It is addressed directly to the Pharisees standing outside, listening.

## Key Idea

_The father in Luke 15 runs toward the returning son before the speech is finished. The welcome of God does not wait for the conditions to be right, it moves first._

## Reflection

Which son do you identify with more? The one who went away and came back, or the one who stayed and resented? What does the father's response to each son say to the place you are in right now?

## Today's Practice

Read Luke 15:20 three times. The father saw him when he was still a long way off. Write a prayer that begins with that image of the Father watching the road, seeing you, running. Let it shape the way you approach God today.
