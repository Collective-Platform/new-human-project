## Reading Notes

Surrender is not **giving up.**

In Gethsemane, Jesus prayed: “Father, if You are willing, take this cup from Me; yet not My will, but Yours be done.” \- Luke 22:42

Jesus was honest about what He wanted. He did not pretend the road ahead was easy. Yet after expressing His desire, He surrendered the outcome to His Father.

This teaches us something important about emotional health: **Surrender does not mean you stop caring. It means you stop demanding that life must happen your way for you to be okay.**

Sometimes our greatest emotional struggles come from the gap between **what we hoped would happen** and **what actually happened**.

We hoped the relationship would work. We hoped the opportunity would come. We hoped someone would change. We hoped life would look different by now.

It's okay to hope. It's also okay to grieve when things don't turn out as expected.

Jesus shows us that we can hold **desire and surrender** together: **“Father, this is what I want—but I trust You even if the answer is different.”**

Surrender is not **hopelessness**. Surrender moves our hope from a particular **outcome** to a trustworthy **God**.

The Holy Spirit helps us loosen our grip without losing our faith. We may not know what happens next, but we know Who walks with us.

## Practice

Sit quietly and hold your hands tightly closed. Name the outcome you've been holding onto. Then slowly open your hands.

## Reflection

Is your hope resting in **God**, or mainly in a particular outcome?
