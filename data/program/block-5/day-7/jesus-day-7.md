## Today's Focus

What is the question Jesus puts to every person and what is at stake in the answer?

## Reading Notes

Halfway through Matthew's Gospel, Jesus takes his disciples to Caesarea Philippi, a region associated with the worship of many gods, and asks two questions. The first is about public opinion: Who do people say the Son of Man is? The answers come easily: John the Baptist, Elijah, Jeremiah, or one of the prophets. Then the second question, directed at the disciples personally: But who do you say that I am? Peter answers: You are the Christ, the Son of the living God. Jesus declares it a revelation from the Father, not human insight, but divine disclosure. The question does not go away. Thomas, after the resurrection, sees the risen Jesus and makes the most complete confession in the Gospel of John: My Lord and my God. He moves from doubt to the highest declaration of faith in the entire New Testament. The question Jesus asked at Caesarea Philippi is the question every human being must eventually answer, not as a theological exercise, but as the most personal question anyone can face. Everyone has an opinion about Jesus. The question is not about opinion; it is about a personal, committed, life shaping response: who do you say that I am?

## Key Idea

_Jesus does not ask for our assessment of popular opinion. He asks for our own answer, the one that will shape everything else. Who do you say that I am?_

## Reflection

If Jesus looked you in the eye today and asked, "Who do you say I am?" What would your honest answer be? And if that answer is true, what does it require of your life right now?

## Today's Practice

Write your own answer to Jesus's question not the correct theological answer, but your real, personal, honest answer. Then read it back to him in prayer. Let it be the foundation on which today's practice rests.
