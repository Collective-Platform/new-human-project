## Today's Focus

What does Jesus's washing of the disciples' feet reveal about the kind of leadership and the kind of life his followers are called to?

## Reading Notes

It is the night before the crucifixion. Jesus knows that his hour has come that he is moving toward the cross. And John tells us why he does what he does next: having loved his own who were in the world, he loved them to the end. He gets up from the meal, wraps a towel around his waist, and begins to wash the disciples' feet. This was the work of the lowest household servant, the one too junior to be given any other task. Peter protests: You shall never wash my feet. Jesus responds: if I do not wash you, you have no share with me. And when it is done, he explains: You call me Teacher and Lord, and you are right, for so I am. If I then, your Lord and Teacher, have washed your feet, you also ought to wash one another's feet. For I have given you an example, that you also should do just as I have done to you. He has not stooped to wash their feet despite who he is. He has washed their feet because of who he is. His authority and his servanthood are not in tension. They are the same thing. And this is the shape of life he invites his followers into: not the exercise of power over others, but the freedom of one who is secure enough in their identity that they can kneel before anyone.

## Key Idea

_Jesus washed feet not despite his authority but from it. The freedom to serve without losing dignity comes from knowing who you are and that security is what he offers his followers._

## Reflection

Is there a person or a task in your life that you have considered beneath your dignity to serve? What does the foot-washing scene say to that instinct? What would it look like to kneel before that person or task with the freedom of someone who knows who they are?

## Today's Practice

Identify one act of lowly, unglamorous service you can do for someone today, something that will not be noticed or rewarded. Do it as an act of worship, with the towel of Jesus in mind. Let it be your prayer.
