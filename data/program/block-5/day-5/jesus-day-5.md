## Today's Focus

What does Scripture reveal about the genuine humanity of Jesus and why does it matter that he is truly human and not merely human in appearance?

## Reading Notes

The church has always insisted on two things about Jesus simultaneously: that he is fully God and fully human. The first is easier to celebrate; the second is equally important and equally true. Luke tells us that Jesus grew in wisdom, stature, and favor with God and man. He developed, learned, and increased. He was not born with all knowledge preloaded; he grew as a child grows. At the tomb of Lazarus, he wept, not as a performance of sympathy, but as the expression of a man who loved and felt the weight of loss. The writer of Hebrews insists that Jesus was tempted in every way as we are, yet without sin. The temptations were real, not theatrical. Therefore, we do not have a high priest unable to sympathize with our weaknesses. The sympathy of Jesus is not the detached compassion of someone who has read about suffering from a distance. It is the solidarity of someone who has been inside it, who knows hunger, exhaustion, grief, disappointment, and the particular anguish of being misunderstood by those closest to him.

## Key Idea

_Jesus is not almost human or human in appearance only. He is genuinely, fully human. This means he knows your experience from the inside and can sympathise with a sympathy that is real._

## Reflection

When you think about Jesus being fully human, which part speaks to you most: that he had to learn and grow, that he wept, that he faced real temptation, or that he grew tired? How does knowing he truly understands these feelings change the way you talk to him about your own struggles?

## Today's Practice

Read Hebrews 4:14-16 slowly. The writer calls us to approach the throne of grace with confidence because of Jesus's humanity, not despite it. Write a prayer that brings one specific human struggle to Jesus precisely because he knows it from the inside.
