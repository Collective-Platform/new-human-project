## Today's Focus

What does Jesus's prayer life reveal about his relationship with the Father and what does he teach his disciples to expect from prayer?

## Reading Notes

The Gospels return again and again to a simple fact: Jesus prayed. Not occasionally, not when he needed something, and not as a religious duty. Prayer was the rhythm his life was built around. In the middle of a healing ministry that was attracting enormous crowds, he withdrew to desolate places to pray. After a full day of ministry, he rose before dawn, went to a desolate place, and prayed there. The disciples noticed something different about the way Jesus prayed, something so distinct that they asked him to teach them. What they received was not a technique but a pattern: Father, the intimacy of a son; your kingdom come, the orientation of a life submitted to God's agenda rather than its own; give us today our daily bread, a daily dependence that does not reach beyond the present moment; forgive us as we forgive, the connection between received grace and extended grace; deliver us from evil, the honest acknowledgment that there are forces we cannot overcome alone. Jesus taught prayer not as a formula but as a relationship: the ongoing conversation of a child with a Father who is good, who gives good gifts, and who hears.

## Key Idea

_Prayer was not an addition to Jesus's ministry; it was the source from which it flowed. What he taught his disciples to pray, he lived out daily._

## Reflection

What does your prayer life currently reveal about your relationship with God? Is it a conversation or a performance? Is it daily dependence or occasional petition? What would it look like to pray the way Jesus prayed going from intimacy rather than toward it?

## Today's Practice

Pray the Lord's Prayer today not quickly, but slowly, phrase by phrase. Pause on each petition and ask what it is actually asking of you. Let it take as long as it needs to.
