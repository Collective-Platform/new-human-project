## Today's Focus

What is Jesus claiming in the seven I AM statements of John and what is the significance of the divine name?

## Reading Notes

Seven times in John's Gospel, Jesus uses the words I am followed by a declaration: I am the bread of life; the light of the world; the door; the good shepherd; the resurrection and the life; the way, the truth, and the life; the true vine. Each declaration maps a human need onto himself, the need for sustenance, direction, safety, guidance, life, truth, belonging, and says: I am the answer to that. But the most explosive use of I am in John comes in chapter 8\. When the Pharisees challenge him about his age in relation to Abraham, Jesus says: before Abraham was, I am. The crowd immediately picks up stones because they understand exactly what he has said. I am, ego eimi, is the Greek equivalent of the divine name revealed to Moses at the burning bush: I AM WHO I AM. Jesus is not saying he existed before Abraham; he is claiming the eternal, self existent being of God. The reaction of the crowd is the reaction of people who have heard correctly. The question is not whether Jesus claimed to be God: the question is whether the claim is true.

## Key Idea

_The I AM declarations of John are not merely poetic. they are claims to divine identity. Jesus maps every human need onto himself and says: I am the source of what you most deeply require._

## Reflection

Which of the seven I AM statements speaks most directly to where you are right now? What does it mean to bring that specific need to the one who declares himself to be its answer?

## Today's Practice

Write out all seven I AM statements from John's Gospel. Next to each one, write one word that names the human need it addresses. Then choose the one most relevant to your life today and pray it as a declaration of trust.
