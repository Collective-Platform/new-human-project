## Today's Focus

What is happening at the cross and how do we hold together its historical reality and its theological depth?

## Reading Notes

The crucifixion account in Mark is told with extraordinary restraint. And they crucified him. Three words in the Greek, describing the most important event in human history without elaboration, dramatic heightening, or interpretation. The interpretation comes from elsewhere. Isaiah 53 had spoken seven centuries earlier of a servant who would be pierced for our transgressions, crushed for our iniquities, upon whom the punishment that brought us peace was laid. Paul distills it: God made him who knew no sin to be sin for us, so that in him we might become the righteousness of God. At the cross, something is happening that is simultaneously historical, a man dying on a Roman execution device, and cosmic, the weight of human sin being absorbed by the one who made humanity and loves them. The darkness that falls from noon to three is not a weather event. It is the sign of something happening beyond what any observer can see. And then the cry: My God, my God, why have you forsaken me? The desolation is real. The abandonment is real. And then: It is finished. Now I am finished, the work is finished. What needed to be done has been done. The curtain in the temple tears from top to bottom. The barrier between human beings and the presence of God is removed, not from the bottom up by human effort, but from the top down by divine action.

## Key Idea

_At the cross, God absorbs what human sin deserves and gives back what it could never earn. It is finished, not as a cry of defeat but as the declaration of a completed work._

## Reflection

What does the cross mean to you not as a doctrine to be affirmed but as a personal reality? What specifically has been finished that you could not finish yourself? Sit with that question today.

## Today's Practice

Read Isaiah 53:4-6 slowly, substituting 'me' and 'my' for every reference to 'us' and 'our'. Read it as your own account. Then read 2 Corinthians 5:21. Write one sentence about what the exchange at the cross means for you specifically.
