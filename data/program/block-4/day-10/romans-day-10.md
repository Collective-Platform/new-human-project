## Today's Focus

_What does it mean that we died with Christ and why does it change everything about how we live?_

## Reading Notes

Shall we go on sinning so that grace may increase? Paul anticipates the objection and dismisses it with the same two words he used in chapter 3: _by no means_. This is the strongest negative available in the Greek language. The reason is simple: we cannot go on living in sin because we have died to it. Baptism into Christ is baptism into his death. The old self was crucified with him and the body of sin was done away with, so that we are no longer enslaved to sin. Paul is not describing a subjective feeling of having died to sin. He is describing an objective reality that is true of every person who belongs to Christ. The question is not whether it happened. It did, at conversion, in union with Christ. The question is whether you know it, count on it, and present yourself to God on that basis. The new humanity does not have to sin. That is the true freedom Paul is describing.

## Key Idea

You do not have to sin. The old self that was enslaved to it has died with Christ. The life you now live is a resurrection life, and sin no longer holds any legitimate authority over you.

## Reflection

Which habitual sin have you accepted as an unchangeable part of your identity? What would it mean today to genuinely count yourself dead to that pattern and alive to God instead?

## Today's Practice

Read Romans 6:11 as a personal declaration: “I consider myself dead to sin and alive to God in Christ Jesus.” Apply it specifically to one pattern of sin you have been treating as inevitable. Say it daily this week.
