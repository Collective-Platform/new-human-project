**Scripture:** Romans 8:1-4 \- Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus, **2** because through Christ Jesus the law of the Spirit who gives life has set you\[[a](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%208%3A1-4&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28119a)\] free from the law of sin and death. **3** For what the law was powerless to do because it was weakened by the flesh,\[[b](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%208%3A1-4&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28120b)\] God did by sending his own Son in the likeness of sinful flesh to be a sin offering.\[[c](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Romans%208%3A1-4&version=NIV#fen-NIV-28120c)\] And so he condemned sin in the flesh, **4** in order that the righteous requirement of the law might be fully met in us, who do not live according to the flesh but according to the Spirit.

One of the biggest lies we often believe is that maturity means carrying everything by ourselves.

We carry expectations—our own and everyone else's. We carry disappointment from past experiences, guilt over our mistakes, pressure to perform, and fear of letting people down. Over time, these invisible burdens become so familiar that we no longer realise how heavy they are.

Yet Romans 8 opens with one of the most freeing declarations in all of Scripture:

> **"Therefore, there is now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus."**

Paul writes these words immediately after Romans 7, where he openly describes his own struggle with sin. He admits that he often does the very things he doesn't want to do. If anyone understood the frustration of feeling stuck, it was Paul.

But Romans 8 changes the conversation. Paul no longer focuses on what he cannot do. Instead, he points to what Christ has already done.

Notice the difference. The Christian life is not built on trying harder—it is built on trusting deeper.

Many of us continue carrying guilt that Jesus has already forgiven. We replay past failures, compare ourselves with others, or believe that our value depends on our performance.

The Holy Spirit gently reminds us that our identity is not built on our success but on God's grace. **Surrender begins when we stop trying to save ourselves.**

This doesn't mean we stop taking responsibility. Rather, it means we stop carrying responsibilities that only God can carry.

Perhaps today you're carrying a burden that has slowly become your identity.

"I have to keep everyone happy."

"I cannot disappoint people."

"I always need to stay strong."

"If I fail, I am a failure."

These are burdens Jesus never intended for you to carry.

The Holy Spirit invites us to exchange self-reliance for God-reliance. Every time we surrender our burdens, we make room for His peace to lead us.

## Practice

Imagine placing a heavy backpack at the feet of Jesus. Name each burden slowly before Him.

## Reflection

What burden have you been carrying for far too long?
