## Today's Focus

_What is the mystery Paul reveals here and what does it tell us about the scope of God's purposes?_

## Reading Notes

“Lest you be wise in your own sight, I do not want you to be unaware of this mystery.”' Paul calls it a mystery, not in the sense of something unknowable, but in the biblical sense of something previously hidden, now revealed. A partial hardening has come upon Israel until the fullness of the Gentiles has come in. And then all Israel will be saved. Paul is not abandoning the gospel or introducing a second path to God. He is describing a trajectory of history in which the purposes of God for Israel are not abandoned but deferred, and ultimately fulfilled. The same God who shut up all people in disobedience has done so in order to have mercy on all. The scope of grace is wider than our theological systems have usually been willing to accommodate. The mercies of God are not our property to distribute. They are his to give, and he gives them with an extravagance that regularly surprises us.

## Key Idea

The purposes of God are wider and longer than our theologies usually allow. He has shut up all in disobedience in order to have mercy on all, and his mercy will have the final word.

## Reflection

Is there a person or group you have mentally written off from the mercy of God, someone you do not expect God to reach? What does Romans 11 verse 32 say to that expectation?

## Today's Practice

Name one person you have given up praying for or someone you believe is beyond God's reach. Bring them back to prayer today, with Romans 11:32 as your foundation: 'God has consigned all to disobedience in order to have mercy on all.'
