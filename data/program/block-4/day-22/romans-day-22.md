## Today's Focus

_What does genuine love look like in the texture of daily community life?_

## Reading Notes

Let love be genuine. The Greek is uncompromising: love without hypocrisy. Not love as a performed virtue, not love as a church value on a vision statement, but love that is real, the same in private as it is in public, the same toward the difficult person as toward the easy one. Paul then gives the most practical description of love in community anywhere in his letters: outdo one another in showing honour, be fervent in spirit, rejoice with those who rejoice and weep with those who weep, live in harmony with one another, do not be haughty, associate with the lowly. And then the most demanding: if your enemy is hungry, feed him. If he is thirsty, give him something to drink. Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good. This is not a list of aspirations. It is the actual daily texture of life in the new humanity, the community that loves as it has been loved.

## Key Idea

Love without hypocrisy means the same in private as in public, the same toward enemies as toward friends. This is the daily texture of life in the new humanity.

## Reflection

Where in your daily life is your love most likely to be hypocritical, present in public, absent in private? Which specific command from Romans 12:9 to 21 most challenges you this week?

## Today's Practice

Pick one command from Romans 12:9–21 and practice it specifically today. Not as a performance, but as a genuine act of love toward a real person in your life. Do it without announcing it.
