## Today's Focus

_What happens when religious privilege becomes a source of pride rather than responsibility?_

## Reading Notes

Paul turns now to the Jewish reader specifically. This is the one who possesses the law, knows God’s will, has been instructed from Scripture, and considers themselves a guide to the blind. Paul does not deny the privileges of Israel. He honours them. But he presses the question: does having the law mean you keep it? Does knowing the Scriptures mean you live by them? The terrible irony he points out is that God’s name is blasphemed among the Gentiles because of the gap between what Israel claims and how Israel lives. Religious privilege that does not produce a changed life is worse than no privilege at all. It discredits the God it claims to represent. For the church today, the application is inescapable: what we believe and how we live must match. The watching world does not judge our theology. It judges our behaviour.

## Key Idea

Religious knowledge and privilege are gifts — but they become a liability when the life they demand is not being lived. The world judges what it sees, not what we profess.

## Reflection

Is there a gap in your community between what you teach and how you actually live? What is one area where your actions need to match your words?

## Today's Practice

Ask one trusted friend outside your church: what impression does our church community give of the God we claim to follow? Listen without defending. Pray about what you hear.
