## Today's Focus

_Why does Paul use the language of slavery to describe both our old life and our new one?_

## Reading Notes

Paul uses an image that would have been instantly understood in first century Rome: slavery. Everyone serves a master. The question is only which one. Before Christ, you were enslaved to sin, not because you chose every sinful act, but because sin had a systemic hold over the direction of your will. In Christ, that mastery has been broken. You have been set free from sin and have become slaves of righteousness. The new slavery is not oppressive. Paul acknowledges the awkwardness of the metaphor, yet it captures something entirely true: you cannot be neutral. Freedom in the gospel is not the absence of obligation. It is the exchange of masters, moving from the master who leads to death to the master who leads to eternal life. The new humanity is a community of people who have chosen and been freed to serve the God who made them.

## Key Idea

Freedom in the gospel is not the ability to do whatever we want. It is a change of masters, moving from the cruel power of sin to the care of the God who loves us.

## Reflection

What does it feel like to think of yourself as a slave of righteousness rather than a free agent? Does that language constrict you or liberate you? Why?

## Today's Practice

Reflect on one area of your life where you still feel enslaved to the wrong master: an old habit, a fear, or a craving. Bring it explicitly under the lordship of Christ today. Ask him to make his mastery real in that specific place.
