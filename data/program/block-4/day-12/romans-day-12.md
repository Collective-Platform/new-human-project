## Today's Focus

_What is Paul describing in Romans 7 and what does it tell us about the ongoing experience of the Christian life?_

## Reading Notes

Romans 7 is one of the most debated passages in all of Paul. Who is the person who wants to do good yet still does evil? Whoever this voice represents, Paul makes his purpose clear. The law is not the problem. The problem is sin operating in human nature. “I delight in the law of God in my inner being, but I see another power waging war inside me.” The experience Paul describes, wanting what is right but doing what is wrong, is what happens when someone cares about goodness but lacks the power of the Spirit. It ends in a desperate cry: “Wretched man that I am\! Who will deliver me from this body of death?” Then comes the triumphant answer: “Thanks be to God through Jesus Christ our Lord\!” Romans 7 is not the final resting place. It is the doorway leading from struggle into the life of the Spirit in chapter 8\.

## Key Idea

Romans 7 shows the failure of human effort without the Spirit. It is a cry for help, and chapter 8 is the answer.

## Reflection

Do you relate more to the struggle of Romans 7 or the freedom of Romans 8 in your daily life? What does the movement between these two chapters suggest about where God is calling you to walk today?

## Today's Practice

Read Romans 7:24 to 25 and then immediately read Romans 8:1 to 2\. Pray the movement between them: “Wretched as I am, thanks be to God through Jesus Christ, because there is now no condemnation for me in him.”
