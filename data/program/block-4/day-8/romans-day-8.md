## Today's Focus

_What is the peace Paul describes and why does it produce joy even in suffering?_

## Reading Notes

Therefore, since we have been justified by faith, we have peace with God through our Lord Jesus Christ. Peace with God is not merely a feeling of religious calm. It is the end of a state of hostility. The war between human self will and divine holiness is over. From this peace flows access. We have gained access to this grace in which we stand. The Greek word for access, “_prosagoge_”, was used for being ushered into the presence of a king. The justified person stands in grace, not on probation. Paul then makes an extraordinary claim: we rejoice in our sufferings. We do this not merely in spite of them, but because of what they produce. Suffering produces endurance, endurance produces character, and character produces hope. This hope does not put us to shame, because God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit. The new humanity is not protected from suffering. It is sustained within suffering by a love that never runs dry.

## Key Idea

Peace with God is not a feeling. It is a legal reality with personal consequences. The war is over. You stand in grace. And from that place, even suffering becomes a path to hope.

## Reflection

Do you live from a deep security that you stand in grace, or does your feeling of God's acceptance rise and fall with your daily performance? What would it mean today to rest in grace rather than rely on your own record?

## Today's Practice

Every morning this week, begin the day with this declaration: “I have peace with God through Jesus Christ. I stand in grace.” Not as a feeling to manufacture, but as a truth to receive. Notice what it does.
