## Today's Focus

_What is the difference between seeking righteousness by law and receiving it by faith and why does it matter so much?_

## Reading Notes

Israel pursued righteousness but did not attain it, not because they lacked effort, but because they pursued it as if it were a matter of works rather than faith. They stumbled over the stumbling stone, Christ himself. And then Paul gives one of his most personal and passionate summaries of the gospel: “The word of faith that we proclaim: if you confess with your mouth that Jesus is Lord and believe in your heart that God raised him from the dead, you will be saved.” The heart that believes, the mouth that confesses. Not a complex religious programme, not an ethnic heritage, not a moral achievement. Faith and confession, available to everyone, for everyone who calls on the name of the Lord will be saved. This is the wide open door of the gospel, set against the background of Israel's tragic attempt to earn what can only be received.

## Key Idea

Righteousness cannot be achieved by effort, it can only be received by faith. The door is wide open to everyone, without any condition except trust.

## Reflection

How much of your daily Christian life is lived in the mode of earning rather than receiving? What would it look like to live today entirely from received righteousness?

## Today's Practice

Pray Romans 10:9 as your own confession today. Not for the first time, but as a fresh act of trust: 'Jesus, you are Lord. God raised you from the dead. I am trusting you today, not my own effort.'
