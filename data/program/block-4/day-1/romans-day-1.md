## Today's Focus

_What is Paul actually claiming when he says the gospel is the power of God for salvation?_

## Reading Notes

Paul begins his most carefully argued letter with a declaration that would have sounded absurd in Rome: I am not ashamed of the gospel. In a city of military power, philosophical achievement, and imperial grandeur, Paul is staking his life on the claim that a crucified Jewish teacher from the margins of the empire is the fulfilment of God's ancient promise and the hope of every human being. The gospel he describes is not a self-help programme or a religious system. It is power, which is the same Greek word used for dynamite; the active, life-giving force of God unleashed in the world through Jesus. And the logic of this power is the righteousness of God revealed from faith to faith. A great insight here is that 'the righteousness of God' is not primarily about our own standing before God, but about God's own faithfulness to his covenant promises. The good news is that God has kept his word and that has changed everything.

## Key Idea

The gospel is not good advice or good feelings. It is the power of God and God's own faithfulness to his promises that is unleashed in human history through Jesus.

## Reflection

Where are you most tempted to soften the gospel? What does that reveal about where your real confidence lies?

## Today's Practice

In one sentence, write out your definition of the gospel. Is it the full power of God, or have you reduced it to something smaller? Bring that answer to God in prayer.
