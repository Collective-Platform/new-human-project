# A Word Before You Begin

We have been on this road for a while now. In **Ephesians** we saw the blueprint of the new humanity, who God has made us in Christ, what community he is building and how our identity reshapes every corner of life. In **Philippians** we saw that vision tested by real life: joy under pressure, humility in conflict and peace that passes understanding. In our study of the **Holy Spirit** we met the person who makes all of it possible. Not a power to be channelled but a divine person to be known; who forms Christ in us from the inside out.

Now we come to **Romans**. And if the previous three studies were building the house, Romans is the foundation beneath the foundation. One the most carefully constructed, theologically dense, and ultimately most liberating document Paul ever wrote.

Romans is Paul’s great argument for the gospel. Rather than a summary of Christian beliefs, it explains what God accomplished through Jesus Christ and what that means for all humanity, the story of Israel and the nations, and how we live in response to this news.

It is not an easy letter. Known as the fullest and most systematic explanation of the gospel in Scripture, it has sparked every major movement of church renewal in history, from Augustine and Luther to Wesley and beyond. Whenever the church has lost its way, Romans have called it home.

These 25 days won't cover everything; no study plan could. Instead, they will guide you through the letter’s great movements: from human need and God's overwhelming grace, to His larger purpose in history and what a life fully given to Him looks like.

This study draws on trusted evangelical Bible scholars, experts who treat Scripture as God’s trustworthy Word. But the heart of every page remains the text of Romans itself, read closely, explained clearly, and applied honestly.

You are not beginning this study as a neutral observer. You are already part of the new humanity this letter describes. Romans will tell you more fully what that means, and call you more completely into it.

# Four Movements

The letter falls into four natural movements. Each builds on the last, moving from the diagnosis of human need, through the breathtaking sufficiency of grace, into the mystery of God's purposes in history, and out into the practical shape of a life given entirely to God.

| **Romans 1–4** Romans begins not with comfort but with diagnosis. Paul is writing to a church in the capital of the world's most powerful empire, a city of culture, religion, law, and achievement. And his opening claim is staggering: every human being, without exception, stands before God in the same condition. Jew and Gentile, moral and immoral, religious and irreligious, all have fallen short. The good news of Romans cannot be heard properly until the bad news has been faced honestly. These seven days follow Paul's careful argument from the universality of human need to the breathtaking sufficiency of God's solution: righteousness given as a gift, received through faith, demonstrated at the cross. | **Romans 5–8** Chapters 5 through 8 are the summit of Romans, perhaps the summit of all Paul's writing. Having established that every human being is justified freely by grace through faith, Paul now unfolds what that justified life actually looks like from the inside. It is a life of peace with God, of freedom from sin's power, of death to the old self and resurrection with Christ, of walking not in the flesh but in the Spirit. These seven days are not primarily about doctrine to be understood but about a life to be entered, and the greatest of these chapters, Romans 8, is one of the most beautiful and hope-filled passages in all of Scripture. | **Romans 9–11** Romans chapters 9 through 11 is the section most readers skip; it is the section most preachers avoid. It is also, in many readings, the theological heart of the whole letter. Paul is wrestling with the most urgent question raised by the gospel: if God made promises to Israel, and Israel has largely rejected her Messiah, has God's word failed? The answer Paul gives is one of the most sophisticated and pastorally important in all of Scripture. Remarkably, it ends not in a systematic conclusion but in a doxology. These five days slow down with Paul in these chapters and ask what they mean for the new humanity's understanding of God, of history, and of our own place in a story that is far larger than we imagined. | **Romans 12–16** Romans 12 begins with therefore, and that word carries the weight of eleven chapters. Because of everything God has done, justifying the ungodly, uniting Jew and Gentile, working all things together for His purposes, separating no one from His love, therefore, present your bodies as a living sacrifice. The final movement of Romans is not an appendix of practical tips. It is the outworking of the gospel in every dimension of life: in worship, in community, in the use of gifts, in relationships with outsiders and enemies, in submission to authority, in the welcome of the weak. The new humanity that God has been building across eleven chapters now has to be lived out, in Rome, in every city, in your church, today. |
