## Today's Focus

_Why does Paul end his most demanding theological argument not with a summary but with worship?_

## Reading Notes

“Oh, the depth of the riches and wisdom and knowledge of God\! How unsearchable are his judgments and how inscrutable his ways\!” After three chapters of wrestling with some of the most difficult theological questions in Scripture, election, Israel, Gentile inclusion, the mystery of history, Paul does not conclude with a systematic summary. He concludes with worship. His argument has driven him not to intellectual satisfaction but to doxology. This is the mark of genuine theological engagement: when you think deeply enough about God, you do not end with pride in your conclusions. You end on your knees. For all things are from him and through him and to him. To him be glory forever. The relationship of the new humanity with theology is not primarily academic. It is non-academic, thinking hard about God in order to worship him more truly and more deeply.

## Key Idea

The right response to the deepest theology is not satisfaction, but worship. When you have truly thought about God, you end not with conclusions, but with praise.

## Reflection

When did you last find theology driving you to worship rather than just to more head knowledge? What would it look like to hold your deepest convictions with humble wonder rather than intellectual pride?

## Today's Practice

Read Romans 11:33–36 slowly, out loud, as a personal act of worship. Then sit in silence for five minutes. Let the unsearchable wisdom of God be larger than your understanding of it.
