## Today's Focus

_Why does Paul begin the gospel letter with such a dark diagnosis of humanity?_

## Reading Notes

This is one of the most uncomfortable passages in Paul, and one of the most honest. The wrath of God is being revealed from heaven against all ungodliness and unrighteousness. Paul is not describing an angry temperament in God. He is describing the moral structure of the universe. God has made Himself known through creation, yet human beings have systematically suppressed that knowledge and exchanged the truth for a lie. The result is not immediate punishment from the outside. It is something far more sobering: God gave them up. Paul uses this exact phrase three times to describe God releasing people to the consequences of their own choices. The darkness Paul describes is the natural destination of a world that turns away from the light it was given. This is not pleasant reading, but without it, the gospel has nothing to save us from. The new humanity is not a self improvement project; it is a rescue from a burning building.

## Key Idea

The darkness Paul describes in Romans 1 is not God's arbitrary judgment. It is what happens when a world made for God turns away from him and is given up to its own direction.

## Reflection

How does Romans 1 change your view of the gospel? Do you see it only as forgiveness for specific sins, or as rescue from a deeper condition?

## Today's Practice

Read Romans 1:21–23. Where have you failed to glorify God or give Him thanks? Name one area where your thinking has become futile, or where you have exchanged His glory for something created.
