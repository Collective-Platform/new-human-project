## Today's Focus

_How does Paul's handling of disagreement within the church model what the new humanity should look like?_

## Reading Notes

Welcome the one who is weak in faith, but not to quarrel over opinions. Paul is dealing with a real, pastoral conflict in the Roman church: Jewish believers who observe food laws and special days, and Gentile believers who do not. Neither side is wrong in principle. But each is tempted to judge or despise the other. Paul’s instruction is remarkable for its pastoral wisdom: the strong should bear the failings of the weak and not please themselves. Welcome one another as Christ has welcomed you. The measure of welcome is not agreement on secondary issues. It is the welcome of Christ himself, the one who received you not because you had your theology perfectly sorted, but because he loved you. The new humanity is not a community of people who agree on everything. It is a community of people who have learned to welcome each other the way they themselves have been welcomed by God.

## Key Idea

The new humanity is not held together by agreement on secondary issues. It is held together by the welcome of Christ, who received each of us not because we deserved it, but because he loved us.

## Reflection

What are the secondary issues in your church that cause the most division? How would Paul's 'welcome one another as Christ has welcomed you' apply to your specific situation?

## Today's Practice

Identify one person in your church with whom you disagree on a secondary matter: worship style, theology, or practice. Find one way to genuinely welcome them this week, not despite the disagreement, but through the welcome of Christ that overarches it.
