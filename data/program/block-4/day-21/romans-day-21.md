## Today's Focus

_Why does Paul's call to sober self-assessment come immediately after his call to consecration?_

## Reading Notes

“Do not think of yourself more highly than you ought to think, but think with sober judgment.” This is the virtue of measured, accurate self knowledge. Not false humility, not self deprecation, but honest realism about who you are and what you have been given. And what follows immediately is the body metaphor: we, though many, are one body in Christ, and individually members one of another. Having many different gifts, prophecy, service, teaching, exhortation, giving, leading, mercy, the call is simply to use each one according to the grace given. Not to compare, not to compete, not to wish for someone else's gift, but to use what you have been given with whatever quality that gift demands: the one who gives with generosity, the one who leads with zeal, the one who does acts of mercy with cheerfulness. The new humanity is built by each person contributing what they have actually been given.

## Key Idea

Sober, honest self knowledge, knowing what you have been given and using it, is more useful to the new humanity than impressive gifts used for self promotion.

## Reflection

What gifts has God genuinely given you, and are you using them, or comparing them unfavourably to someone else's? What would it look like to use what you have with the quality Paul describes?

## Today's Practice

Identify one gift you have been given, however modest it seems, that you have not been using fully. Take one concrete step this week to use it in service of someone else.
