## Today's Focus

_What is Paul's deep anguish at the beginning of chapter 9 and what is his answer to it?_

## Reading Notes

“I have great sorrow and unceasing anguish in my heart.” Paul is not theorising in Romans 9 to 11\. He is in pain. His own people, Israel, the nation entrusted with the oracles of God, from whom the Messiah came according to the flesh, have largely rejected that Messiah. Has the word of God failed? Paul’s answer is carefully layered. Not all Israel is Israel. God’s election was never about ethnicity alone, for it was always about the God who calls, who has mercy on whom he has mercy. Paul ranges across Scripture, the election of Jacob over Esau, the hardening of Pharaoh, the potter and the clay, to make one centralised point: God is not capricious, but he is sovereign. His purposes cannot be thwarted by human unbelief. The question ‘has the word of God failed?’ must be answered ‘no’, but the reasons why require careful listening.

## Key Idea

God's purposes are not derailed by human unbelief. His word does not fail. But understanding how requires entering a story much larger than our own immediate experience.

## Reflection

Is there a situation in your life or church where the purposes of God seem to have paused, where you wonder if his promise has lost its power? How does Paul’s confidence in the sovereignty of God speak to that situation today?

## Today's Practice

Meditate on Romans 9:16: 'It depends not on human will or exertion, but on God, who has mercy.' Where are you trying to force a result in your own strength? Pray right now and explicitly release that burden to his mercy.
