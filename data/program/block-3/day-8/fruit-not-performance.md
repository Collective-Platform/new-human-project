## Today's Focus

Why does Paul use the image of fruit and what does it tell us about how character is formed?

## Reading Notes

The Spirit produces fruit. Not achievements, not performances, not impressive displays. Fruit. And fruit has a particular quality: it cannot be manufactured. It grows slowly, invisibly, from the inside out, in season. You cannot force an apple tree to produce apples faster by trying harder. You can only tend the conditions (light, water, soil) and wait.

Paul's list of the Spirit's fruit: love, joy, peace, patience, kindness, goodness, faithfulness, gentleness, self-control, is a description of a person who looks like Jesus. That is the Spirit's agenda: to form Christ in you. And it happens the way fruit grows: slowly, in hidden conditions, through sustained connection to the vine. Jesus himself said it plainly: 'Abide in me, and I in you. As the branch cannot bear fruit by itself, unless it abides in the vine, neither can you' (John 15:4).

## Key Idea

The Spirit's deepest work is not the spectacular: it is the slow, hidden formation of a character that looks like Jesus.

## Reflection

If someone who knows you well were to assess the fruit in your life, which would they say is most evident? Which would they say needs the most growth?

## Today's Practice

Read Galatians 5:22-23 slowly. Pick one aspect of the fruit and ask: what would it look like to see this grow in me this week in one specific relationship? Commit to it.
