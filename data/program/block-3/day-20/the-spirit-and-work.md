## Today's Focus

The Spirit was given to craftsmen and builders before he was given to preachers. Ordinary work is his domain.

## Reading Notes

One of the most overlooked passages about the Holy Spirit: I have filled Bezalel with the Spirit of God, with skill, with intelligence, with knowledge, and with all craftsmanship. The first person described in Scripture as filled with the Spirit is not a priest or a prophet. He is an artist and craftsman, called to build the tabernacle with beauty and excellence. The Spirit was given to make beautiful things.

This means the Spirit is present in every form of faithful human work: the teacher, the engineer, the nurse, the farmer, the parent, the programmer. Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord.

The new humanity does not have a sacred-secular divide. The Spirit sanctifies ordinary work by inhabiting the person who does it with care, skill, and love..

## Key Idea

The first Spirit-filled person in the Bible was an artist. The Spirit is present in all faithful work, not just in ministry.

## Reflection

Do you think of your daily work as a place where the Spirit is present and active? What would change if you genuinely believed that?

## Today's Practice

Before you begin work tomorrow, pray: 'Spirit, you are here. Be present in what I do today.' At the end of the day, notice one moment where you sensed his presence or cooperation.

The Spirit and the Community

What the Spirit is building and the gifts he uses to build it

1 Corinthians 12–14 · Acts 2 · Romans 12

We arrive finally at the gifts, not because they are unimportant, but because they can only be understood rightly from where we now stand. The gifts of the Spirit are not the summit of spiritual life. They are tools given by the sovereign Spirit to build the community he is forming. These final five days place the gifts in their proper context: not as marks of spiritual superiority, but as acts of service in the new humanity. And they end with the vision of what the Spirit is building, a community that displays the character of Christ to the world.
