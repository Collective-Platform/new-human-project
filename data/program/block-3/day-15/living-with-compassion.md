**Colossians 3:12-14**

12 Therefore, as God’s chosen people, holy and dearly loved, clothe yourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness and patience. 13 Bear with each other and forgive one another if any of you has a grievance against someone. Forgive as the Lord forgave you. 14 And over all these virtues put on love, which binds them all together in perfect unity.

## Reading Notes

One of the greatest signs of emotional maturity is not how well you manage yourself. It is how well you respond to others.

The world often teaches us to protect ourselves first. We are encouraged to guard our time, defend our opinions, and prioritize our own needs. While healthy boundaries are important, there is a danger that self-protection slowly turns into self-preoccupation. We become so consumed with our own struggles that we lose the ability to notice the struggles of those around us.

Compassion invites us to look beyond ourselves.

The word compassion literally means "to suffer with." It is more than feeling sorry for someone. It is choosing to enter another person's pain with love, grace, and presence. Throughout the Gospels, this is exactly how Jesus lived. Again and again, we are told that He was "moved with compassion." Before He healed the sick, fed the hungry, or comforted the grieving, His heart was first moved toward them.

Compassion begins with seeing people the way Jesus sees them.

When someone speaks harshly, our first instinct is often to react. Yet compassion pauses long enough to ask, "I wonder what pain they might be carrying?"

When someone disappoints us, compassion reminds us that every person is fighting battles we may never fully understand.

This does not mean we excuse sin or ignore unhealthy behaviour. Jesus never compromised truth. But He always held truth together with grace. He confronted people without condemning them. He corrected without humiliating. He welcomed without lowering God's standard.

Paul reminds us that compassion is not merely a personality trait—it is something we intentionally "put on." Just as we choose our clothes each morning, we choose to clothe ourselves with compassion, kindness, humility, gentleness, and patience.

These qualities do not appear naturally. They are formed in us as the Holy Spirit reshapes our hearts. Notice where Paul begins. He does not say, "Try harder to be compassionate." He first reminds us who we are: God's chosen people, holy and dearly loved.

This is the foundation of emotional generosity.

People who are secure in God's love do not need to compete for significance. They are free to celebrate others. They are free to forgive. They are free to listen instead of always needing to be heard. They are free to give grace because they know they have first received grace.

Compassion is not weakness. It is strength that chooses love over retaliation.

## Practice

Intentionally slow down enough to notice someone around you. Ask yourself:

Who seems weary?

Who may be carrying an unseen burden?

Who might simply need someone to listen?

## Reflection

Am I quicker to judge or to understand?
