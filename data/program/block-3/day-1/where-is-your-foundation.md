**Matthew 7:24–27**

24 “Therefore everyone who hears these words of mine and puts them into practice is like a wise man who built his house on the rock. 25 The rain came down, the streams rose, and the winds blew and beat against that house; yet it did not fall, because it had its foundation on the rock. 26 But everyone who hears these words of mine and does not put them into practice is like a foolish man who built his house on sand. 27 The rain came down, the streams rose, and the winds blew and beat against that house, and it fell with a great crash.”

## Reading Notes

Every building is eventually tested.

It is easy to admire a beautiful house on a sunny day. Strong walls, a well-designed roof, and a polished exterior can give the impression that everything is secure. But the true strength of a house is never revealed during calm weather. It is revealed when the wind begins to blow, the rain pours down, and the floodwaters rise.

Jesus uses this picture to teach us an important truth about our lives. Storms are not a matter of if; they are a matter of when. Every one of us will experience disappointment, loss, conflict, uncertainty, failure, or grief. There will be seasons when our emotions feel unsettled, and our hearts feel weary.

The difference is not whether storms come. The difference is what our lives are built upon.

Many of us unknowingly build our emotional lives on foundations that cannot sustain us. We build on success, hoping achievement will make us secure. We build on others' approval, believing acceptance will give us worth. We build on comfort, control, or predictable circumstances, thinking they will keep us safe.

These foundations may seem strong when life is going well. But when circumstances change, they begin to crack. A difficult conversation, a disappointing diagnosis, an unexpected failure, or a broken relationship can quickly expose what we have been depending on all along.

Jesus invites us to build differently. He calls us to build our lives on Him—His words, His character, and His promises. A life rooted in Christ does not avoid storms, but they do not destroy it. The winds may shake us. The rain may soak us. The floods may rise around us. Yet because our foundation is secure, we remain standing.

Emotional resilience is not pretending to be strong or refusing to acknowledge pain. It is learning to anchor your heart in something that does not change when your circumstances do.

The more deeply your roots grow into Christ, the steadier your heart becomes. His love does not change when you fail. His presence does not disappear when you feel anxious. His promises remain true even when your emotions fluctuate.

## Practice

Take ten minutes today to reflect on the foundations of your life.

Complete these sentences honestly:

I feel most secure when...

I feel most shaken when...

I tend to depend on... more than I depend on God.

## Reflection

Are there foundations in my life that are unstable or temporary?
