## Today's Focus

The Spirit's peace is not passivity, it is a settled security that changes the atmosphere around you.

## Reading Notes

To set the mind on the Spirit is life and peace. Peace is not the absence of conflict or pressure. It is the Spirit's deep shalom (a wholeness, a rightness, a settled-ness) that can be present even in the middle of a storm. And notice: it is a fruit of the Spirit's presence in the mind.

The person who sets their mind on the Spirit carries a quality that others feel. They remain steady. They do not escalate. They can hold tension without collapsing or attacking.

Paul's word for peace (eirene) is the New Testament equivalent of shalom. It is not merely the absence of trouble. It is the presence of God making everything right in the soul first, and then spreading outward into the relationships around it.

## Key Idea

The Spirit's peace is not a feeling you seek. It is a quality of life, a settled security, that the Spirit grows in you as you set your mind on him.

## Reflection

Do people around you experience you as a calming presence or a reactive one? What would it look like to be a person who brings the Spirit's peace into difficult situations?

## Today's Practice

The next time you enter a tense situation today (a meeting, a conversation, a family moment) pause for ten seconds before engaging. Breathe. Ask the Spirit for his peace. Then enter.
