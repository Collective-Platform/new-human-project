**Isaiah 30:15**

This is what the Sovereign Lord, the Holy One of Israel, says: “In repentance and rest is your salvation, in quietness and trust is your strength, but you would have none of it.

## Reading Notes

We often think peace comes when our circumstances finally settle down—when the workload eases, the conflict ends, the finances improve, or the future becomes clearer. Yet Scripture presents a different picture. God tells His people that their strength is found not in striving harder, but in quietness and trust.

Our world constantly invites us to react. Every notification, conversation, disappointment, and unexpected challenge competes for our attention. Before we realize it, our hearts become restless, our minds race with "what ifs," and our emotions begin to dictate our decisions. Peace is slowly replaced by anxiety, fear, or frustration.

Biblical peace is not simply the absence of problems. It is the deep confidence that God is present, sovereign, and at work even when life feels uncertain. Peace is something we intentionally guard because what fills our hearts eventually shapes the way we live.

Learning to guard your peace means becoming aware of what steals it. Sometimes it is an overloaded schedule. Sometimes it is comparison. Sometimes it is unresolved hurt or the habit of constantly replaying negative thoughts. Peace is rarely lost all at once—it is often surrendered one small compromise at a time.

The Holy Spirit plays an essential role in this journey. He gently reminds us to slow down before we react, to bring our anxious thoughts before God, and to return our attention to His presence. As we surrender our worries to Him, He produces a peace that is deeper than positive thinking—a peace rooted in God's unchanging character.

## Practice

Take five minutes today to sit quietly without distractions. Breathe slowly and pray: "Holy Spirit, quiet my heart. Help me trust You more than my fears. Teach me to receive Your peace today."

Whenever you notice anxiety rising throughout the day, pause before reacting and whisper, "Lord, You are my peace."

## Reflection

What has been stealing my peace recently?
