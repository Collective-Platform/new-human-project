## Today's Focus

The Spirit's love in us is not a feeling. It is a transformative presence that makes us capable of what we were not before.

## Reading Notes

God's love has been poured into our hearts through the Holy Spirit. The Greek word Paul uses ekcheo means to pour out abundantly, to flood. The Spirit does not produce a mild affection in us. He pours the love of God into us: a love that is patient, kind, not irritable, not resentful, bearing all things. And then Paul says the most searching thing: without this love, tongues, prophecy, knowledge, even faith that moves mountains are nothing.

This is not a gentle correction but a radical reordering of priorities. The most Spirit-filled person in the room is not necessarily the most gifted. It is the most loving. And love, unlike many spiritual expressions, cannot be performed for an audience. It shows in how you treat the difficult person, the overlooked person, the person who has nothing to offer you.

## Key Idea

The Spirit's greatest gift is not tongues or prophecy or healing. It is love. You can tell he is at work in a community by how they treat the people who are hardest to love.

## Reflection

Who in your life is hardest for you to love? What would it look like to ask the Spirit to pour his love into you specifically for that person?

## Today's Practice

Today, do one act of love for someone you find difficult without telling anyone. Do it as an act of cooperation with the Spirit, not as a performance of virtue.
