## Design Specificity Verdict

Auth, onboarding, dashboard, community, and settings carry disparate button radii, hard borders, generic card patterns, and tiny labels. ## Overall Impression

The public entry earns belief: it feels spiritually grounded and visually alive. The member app has thoughtful foundations but currently makes daily practice feel like managing a dashboard rather than returning to a trusted ritual. The highest-leverage opportunity is to make the bilingual daily-practice flow explicit, calm, and safe from accidental state changes.

## What's Working

- The Mental/Emotional/Physical system has real semantic consistency in task lists, charts, feed badges, and icons—this is meaningful product language, not decorative colour.
- The member shell is genuinely mobile-PWA aware: safe-area bottom navigation, constrained reading width, and skeleton/loading support are well considered.
- The landing's video, clear three-part proposition, and concrete programme mockups make Rhythm feel more tangible than a generic habit tracker.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Bilingual support is structurally incomplete.** `app/layout.tsx` fixes the document language as English while the Chinese type variant depends on `html[lang="zh"]`; Chinese routes also expose hard-coded English across landing, login, signup, verification, onboarding, profile, and admin.

**Why it matters:** Chinese members receive a visibly second-class first-run experience and assistive technologies announce the wrong language. This undermines a core product promise before a member begins.

**Fix:** Set document language from the locale, move every visible string and aria-label to `messages`, and test `/en` and `/zh` screen-by-screen, including type fallback and overflow.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

**[P1] “Next” can silently complete personally meaningful work.** In `progress/task-detail.tsx:76`, navigation invokes completion before progressing or closing.

**Why it matters:** A member reading a devotional can create an inaccurate record just by moving through content. Habit data becomes untrustworthy exactly when the experience should feel supportive.

**Fix:** Separate “Mark complete” and “Next,” or label the combined action “Complete & next”; show an immediate undo toast.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`

**[P1] Onboarding asks for browser-level trust before demonstrating value.** The post-handle screen offers install and notification permission alongside Continue, with competing visual treatments and generic copy.

**Why it matters:** Permission prompts are easy to decline or abandon before a user has felt the value of a daily rhythm; “friend requests and activity” is weaker motivation than a daily practice reminder.

**Fix:** Take members to today's first practice immediately. Ask for notifications after their first completion, framed around a chosen daily rhythm; make installation a deferrable tip.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`

**[P2] Daily-practice hierarchy is diffuse.** The progress flow combines a 25-day selector, three equal task cards, row taps, small completion controls, and later add controls, without one dominant next action.

**Why it matters:** Members must work out how to proceed rather than simply returning to a meaningful daily ritual.

**Fix:** Put “Continue today” first, summarize each category state beside it, and make open, complete, and edit actions visibly and verbally distinct.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`

**[P2] Community interaction and privacy cues are under-signalled.** Feed cards combine open-on-tap, like-on-double-tap, and a separate like icon; double tap is undiscoverable. Mood activity receives no nearby privacy explanation.

**Why it matters:** Accidental likes and uncertainty about emotional-data sharing reduce trust in the community surface.

**Fix:** Use explicit labeled controls; remove double tap as a primary interaction; explain what activity becomes visible and offer privacy control at the moment of first sensitive entry.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify`

## Persona Red Flags

**Lin, Chinese first-timer:** `/zh` routes reach English landing, login, OTP, setup, and profile strings; the document language remains English and the intended Chinese type treatment cannot activate. She cannot reasonably trust that the programme supports her language.

**Maya, privacy-sensitive emotional tracker:** She is asked to record feelings and then sees a community feed, but neither the logger nor relevant community state explains what is private or what appears to friends.

**Alex, returning member:** A navigation action can complete a devotional, the 25-day carousel gives no rapid revisit path, and double-tap liking in the feed is invisible until accidentally discovered.

## Minor Observations

- `landing/page.tsx:35` has `text- xl`, so the intended hero-support type size is unlikely to apply.
- `day-carousel.tsx:110` hard-codes “Day”; `dashboard-client.tsx:36` hard-codes `Block N`.
- Profile hard-codes Friends, Settings, Share profile, and View logs; activity-feed hard-codes like-modal text.
- The dashboard radar chart lacks a title or time-frame, weakening its usefulness as a decision aid.
- Preserve 9px chart labels only as a documented chart-specific exception; 10–13px navigation and interactive labels should move to the documented 12/14px minimums.
